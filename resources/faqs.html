Frequently Asked Questions

Plain-language explanations of common terms — general information only, not legal advice for your specific matter.

Deemed Conveyance & Title

What is deemed conveyance?

Deemed conveyance is a legal mechanism under the Maharashtra Ownership Flats Act (MOFA), 1963 that allows a Co-operative Housing Society to obtain title to its land and building from the promoter/developer through the Competent Authority, in cases where the promoter has failed or refused to execute a conveyance voluntarily.

What is Section 11 of MOFA?

Section 11 of MOFA sets out the promoter's obligation to convey title to the Society within the prescribed period, and is the provision under which deemed conveyance applications are filed when that obligation is not met.

Redevelopment

What is the difference between self-redevelopment and builder-led redevelopment?

In self-redevelopment, the Society itself acts as the developer, engaging a PMC, architect, contractor and bank financing directly. In builder-led redevelopment, the Society appoints an external developer, selected typically through a tender process, who executes the project and shares outcomes (additional area, corpus, rent) with the Society as agreed.

What is a corpus fund?

The corpus fund is a lump-sum amount paid by the developer (or set aside in self-redevelopment) to the Society or its members, typically at specified milestones, as consideration for the redevelopment and the temporary disruption it involves.

What is Section 79A?

Section 79A of the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 empowers the State Government to issue binding directives to Societies, including on the process a Society must follow when inviting and evaluating redevelopment tenders. The specific directive/circular in force should be verified for your matter, as these are updated from time to time.

MahaRERA

What is MahaRERA and when does it apply to redevelopment?

MahaRERA is the regulatory authority under the Maharashtra Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016. A redevelopment project ordinarily requires MahaRERA registration once it meets the Act's threshold criteria (built-up area and unit count), and registration brings obligations on project disclosures, escrow of funds, and timelines.

What can a Society do if a developer defaults after registration?

A Society or its members may approach MahaRERA with a complaint, and in appropriate cases pursue a recovery application under Section 40 of the Act for enforcement of a MahaRERA order.

Society Meetings & Governance

What is the difference between an AGM and an SGM/GBM?

An Annual General Meeting (AGM) is the Society's yearly statutory meeting for matters such as adopting accounts and appointing an auditor. A Special General Body Meeting (SGM), sometimes called a General Body Meeting (GBM) called for a specific purpose, is convened outside the AGM cycle to approve a specific resolution, such as developer appointment for redevelopment.

What is the Managing Committee's role during redevelopment?

The Managing Committee typically negotiates and recommends terms, but major decisions — particularly developer/PMC appointment and the Development Agreement — ordinarily require approval by the General Body, not the Committee alone. The specific approval thresholds should be checked against your Society's bye-laws.

Redevelopment Documentation & Approvals

What is FSI and what is TDR?

Floor Space Index (FSI) is the ratio of permissible built-up area to plot area under applicable development control regulations. Transfer of Development Rights (TDR) is additional development potential, generated elsewhere, that can be loaded onto a plot subject to regulatory limits. Both determine how much a Society's plot can be redeveloped to.

What is a Permanent Alternate Accommodation Agreement (PAAA)?

A PAAA is the individual agreement executed between the developer and each member, recording the specific flat/unit the member will receive after redevelopment, its area, and other commercially agreed terms such as rent during construction. It sits alongside the Society-level Development Agreement.

What is a registered Development Agreement and why does it matter?

The Development Agreement is the primary contract between the Society and the developer, setting out scope, timelines, corpus, rent, additional area and other commercial terms. Registration with the Sub-Registrar gives it legal enforceability and public record status, and members should have it reviewed before the General Body approves it.

What is the difference between a Commencement Certificate (CC) and an Occupation Certificate (OC)?

A Commencement Certificate is the municipal authority's permission to begin construction as per the sanctioned plan. An Occupation Certificate (or Completion Certificate) is issued after construction is completed in accordance with that plan, certifying the building is fit for occupation. A Society should not move in, or release final dues, before the OC is in hand.

What documents does a member typically need to submit during redevelopment?

Commonly requested documents include the share certificate, the original agreement for sale/allotment letter, proof of identity and address, and any prior correspondence regarding the flat. The exact list can vary by Society and project — your Managing Committee or PMC will circulate the specific requirement.

Timelines, Delays & Roles

What is a structural audit and when is it required?

A structural audit is a technical assessment of a building's structural condition, typically carried out by a licensed structural engineer. It is commonly required to establish redevelopment eligibility, to support a self-redevelopment or builder-led proposal, and periodically for ageing buildings under municipal safety regulations.

What happens if the Managing Committee's term has expired during redevelopment?

A Society should hold elections and constitute a validly elected Managing Committee as required under the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 and its bye-laws. Major redevelopment decisions taken by a Committee whose term has lapsed can be open to challenge, so this should be regularised promptly.

What can a Society do if a developer stops work or delays the project?

Options typically include invoking the remedies specified in the Development Agreement (such as penalty clauses or termination rights), pursuing a complaint or recovery application before MahaRERA if the project is registered, and taking legal advice on termination and re-tendering if the delay is prolonged and unresolved.

What is the difference between the roles of PMC, architect and legal consultant?

The architect designs the building and prepares statutory plan-sanction drawings. The legal consultant drafts and vets agreements and represents the Society in proceedings. The PMC (Project Management Consultant) coordinates the overall project — timelines, budgets, approvals and the various consultants and contractors — so the Society deals with one accountable point of contact rather than managing each separately.

Are corpus and rent payments received by members taxable?

This depends on the nature and structure of the payment and current income tax provisions, and is a matter for a chartered accountant to advise on for your specific case — this page does not provide tax advice.
